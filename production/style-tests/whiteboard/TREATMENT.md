# "Day 1" — style test, Whiteboard Explainer

17 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps. Picture only: the finished voice track (`test_audio.wav`) and `timeline.json` are fixed inputs (voice only, no score/foley added so all nine tests compare on the same sound).

## Three candidate structures

1. **Pull back from a tiny drawing, then ride the pavement (chosen).** The pen draws Youssef first, small, alone on a white board. The camera pulls back while three pens build London around him: the scale of "first day in London". Then the pen draws the pavement line to the school and the camera rides it; the dialogue is a two-shot on the board; the embarrassed beat is a push into his face where the eraser wipes his smile and the pen redraws it.
2. **Diary page on the board.** "DAY 1" written as a diary header, events listed down the board like journal entries, each illustrated. Clear, but it is a list, not a scene; the dialogue would be text, not acting.
3. **Two halves of the board (a debate).** Youssef's half (Cairo ghosts → London) and Mia's half drawn simultaneously by two pens until they meet at the door. Nice duet, but 7 s of narration is too short to build two worlds, and the brief wants the arrival.

Chosen: 1. It uses the board's own grammar (pull back for scale, the pen leads the camera, magnets are the only things that move, the eraser rewrites a face) and gives the series a reusable recipe: characters live on **magnet cards** so they can walk; sets are drawn live.

## Logline and arc

A newcomer is drawn into existence on a blank board, London is drawn around him, and the board itself corrects his first English sentence.
Setup (drawn, London appears, he slides proudly to the school) → turn ("I... I Youssef." / "Sorry?") → ending (the eraser wipes his smile; a red caret slips "am" into the gap; pen caps).

## Benchmark (grammar only)

- RSA Animate-style live boards: one board, the camera travels, writing finishes on the word. Learn: pacing of a sped-up hand, regions as map. Don't take: drawings, hands (we have none), layouts.
- Teacher's red-pen correction (a proof-reading caret ^ with the missing word above): the native gesture of a language channel. Learn: it reads instantly. Nothing to copy.

## Characters & colour (three markers, meanings fixed)

- **Navy `#1f2a44`**: structure, the world, outlines, all English lettering (Youssef's jacket is navy hatch: brief colour = ink colour).
- **Red `#c8363a`**: accent = "what matters": his red suitcase, the bus stripe (London), the "am" correction, the blush.
- **Amber `#d98a2b`**: warmth / Mia: the sun, her ginger ponytail, her mustard cardigan. Her teal skirt becomes a navy hatch (fourth colour not allowed).
- Youssef: brown skin = light amber hatch over the face and hands; short black hair and trimmed beard = dense navy fill; navy jacket = navy hatch over a white T-shirt (bare board); jeans = light navy hatch; brown shoes = amber fill under navy outline; red suitcase = red. He lives on a **magnet card** (glossy white card, cast shadow) so he can drop in, walk (the card hops step by step), tremble, and sink with embarrassment. Faces don't lip-flap (a board can't): emotion is acted by the card and by the eraser + pen redrawing his face.
- Mia: drawn straight into the school doorway (she belongs to the place and doesn't move): fair skin = bare board, ginger ponytail = dense amber, mustard cardigan = amber hatch, teal skirt = navy hatch (a fourth marker colour is not allowed), clipboard, a raised waving hand with drawn wave marks.
- Ghost marks on the board are faint grammar leftovers of an earlier lesson ("I am", "you are", "he is"): the board has seen this mistake before.
